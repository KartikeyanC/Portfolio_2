Case study · Logo + website

MyshoptillLogo & website for a cloud POS.

Outcome Shipped a logo and a marketing website, designed and built, where each kind of shop finds its own counter in one scroll.

Myshoptill is a cloud POS and mini ERP for supermarkets, hypermarkets, restaurants, textile shops, pharmacies and service businesses, ready for UAE VAT. I designed its logo, then designed and built the marketing website, live at fabiloom.com, where the product currently appears as iPOS.

The brief

Six businesses, one till.

One platform for six very different counters: a supermarket, a hypermarket, a restaurant, a textile shop, a pharmacy and service businesses like spas. Each one bills, stocks and reports in its own way.

So the page had three jobs. Promise all six at once, in the headline. Let every owner find their own shop inside the product within one scroll. And prove it with the product itself, its dashboards, its sync and its invoices, not with adjectives, with every path ending at a demo.

  1. 1One promise

    One smart POS, built for six businesses, said in the first line.

  2. 2Every shop sees itself

    Six industry cards, each with the workflows only that shop needs.

  3. 3Show, don't tell

    Real-looking dashboards, live numbers and a demo button at every turn.

Four people behind every counter.

The site says it plainly: every feature is built for the person actually using it. So the page speaks to four of them.

Front counter

The cashier

On the page: industry workflows, receipt printing
Shop floor

The manager

Runs the stock and the staff. Wants live inventory, low-stock alerts and discount rules nobody can bend.

On the page: the dashboard, discount control
Back office

The accountant

Closes the books. Wants VAT worked out right and every document ready before the auditor asks.

On the page: tax engine, UAE VAT, tax documents
Signs it off

The owner

Sees every branch at once. Wants proof it plugs into the ERP they already run, and numbers they can trust.

On the page: universal ERP, the AI dashboard

0 05

Key design decisions

Six decisions, one key each.

Decision 01 / 06Six businesses in the headline
Key 1

Six businesses in the headline

The first line makes the whole promise: One Smart POS, Built for Six Powerful Businesses, with the six businesses in the brand gradient. Two buttons sit under it, Book a Demo and Watch Product Tour, and the HQ dashboard follows straight after, so the product is part of the very first screen.

Key 2

Every shop finds its own counter

Six cards, one per business, each with its own illustration and five workflows that only that shop needs: matrix inventory and fabric roll cutting for textiles, batch and expiry tracking for pharmacies, kitchen display and split billing for restaurants. Nobody has to translate a generic POS into their own trade.

Key 3

The dashboard, not adjectives

Instead of claims about real-time insight, the page shows the HQ dashboard, big, on its own soft panel. It drifts up and grows to full size as you scroll past, so it feels like a product you are walking around rather than a picture.

Key 4

One brain, shown working

The ERP section lays out five live cards, Purchasing, Sales, Inventory, Finance and Reports, each with real-looking numbers and an updated-just-now line, all wired to one iPOS Core. You can see the sync, not just read about it.

Key 5

A map of every ERP

For the owner who already runs an ERP: iPOS Core in the middle, lines out to SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Odoo, Zoho, Tally and a custom ERP, each labelled with what travels which way. The answer to "will it fit?" in one picture.

Key 6

Money you can watch

The tax engine fills in a sample invoice by itself, one line every 0.65 seconds, until the 5% VAT and the total land. Next to it, a converter moves to a new currency every 2.6 seconds. Two small demos that make tax and payments feel solved.
