The brief

The plan and the work never met.

The problem

Plans live in one app, timers in another, and the record of what actually happened nowhere. So a day's plan and a day's work never meet, and nobody can say where the hours went.

The idea

One planner where moving a task to Working starts its timer, and every change to a task is kept. The plan is the work log.

  • 01See today in one look
  • 02Add work in plain words
  • 03Lose nothing, even offline

The mark

One letter, three ideas.

The NineFlow mark is the app's own initial, drawn so that each stroke of the N says something about what the app does: the day keeps moving, you press start, and you stay a step ahead.

What the mark had to do

  1. Be NineFlow's own letterOne N for the logo, the app icon and the favicon, so the app looks the same everywhere it shows up.
  2. Say what the app is forNineFlow is a planner, a focus timer and a work log. The shapes stand for the flow of the day, the moment you start, and you.
  3. Work from 512 px down to 16 pxFrom the store icon to a browser tab, it still has to read as an N.
  4. Stay orange in every themeThe app has five colour themes and a dark mode. The logo doesn't change with any of them.
From idea to mark

How the N was built, step by step.

Each step adds one part. The part being added is in full colour; the rest of the mark is faded behind it.

  1. 01 · The letter

    Start from the N

    The mark is NineFlow's own initial. An N is three strokes, a stem, a diagonal and a second stem, and each stroke was given a job from the app instead of decoration.

  2. 02 · The diagonal

    The diagonal becomes the flow

    The diagonal turns into one unbroken ribbon, running from top left to bottom right, the way a day is read. It folds over at the top and the bottom instead of meeting the stems at sharp corners, so the line never stops. That's the tagline in one shape: plan better, flow forward.

  3. 03 · The first stem

    The first stem becomes start

    The left stem is cut to a point facing right, like a play button: press start and the focus timer runs. Its two edges meet at the tip at almost a right angle.

  4. 04 · The second stem

    The second stem holds the dot

    The right stem is a short dark body with a dot above it: you, standing on the flow and one step ahead of it. The dot is also the focus point the whole app is built around. Dot and body share one vertical axis, and a single tangent line sets the gap between them.

  5. 05 · Colour

    Warm on the moving parts

    The ribbon and the dot carry the Sunrise to Ember gradient, #FF9F1C to #F2420E. The two stems stay dark charcoal, so the flow stands out from the shapes that hold it. The logo never changes colour with the app's themes: it stays orange everywhere.

  6. 06 · Small sizes

    Checked at 16 px

    An app logo spends most of its life small: on a home screen, in the taskbar, in a browser tab. At 16 px the mark still reads as an N. Around it, keep clear space at least as wide as the dot.

A

Add work fast

Three ways in, and none of them is a form. Whatever you type or say is shown back, and only saved when you say so.

01 · Quick add

Type it, in plain words

Write it the way you’d say it. “Edit trailer tomorrow 2pm 90m #work !high” fills in the day, time, length, category and priority.

02 · Speak tasks

Say it, many at once

Say several tasks in one go, fix them by voice, and check the list before anything is added.

03 · Command palette

Ctrl + K for everything

One box to add a task, run an action or jump to any task, without touching the mouse.

B

Plan & focus

From a goal weeks away, to tonight's hour of work, to the moment a task is really done.

04 · Plan a goal

An exam in 24 days, every evening planned

Name the goal, pick the date and list the topics. NineFlow spreads the work over your free evenings and shows the plan before adding it.

  • Choose the time per day, the days you can work and a start time.
  • Each topic is split over free days; revision comes at the end and a free day is kept before the exam.
  • Days that are already full are skipped; nothing is added until you press "Add 21 tasks".

Why it mattersA big goal turns into small, dated tasks you can actually start tonight.

05 · Focus timer

Focus, and the time is logged

A Pomodoro timer that runs on the task itself, so the minutes you work are recorded where they belong.

  • Start it from any task, or move a card to Working on the Board.
  • Real working time is added to the task and to Charts, next to the time you planned.
  • Runs full-screen on the phone, with a clear way to end the session.

Why it mattersPlan and reality finally meet: you can see how long things really take.

06 · Checklist & finish check

Finish properly

Break a task into steps. If you try to complete it with steps still open, NineFlow asks first.

  • Add checklist steps to any task and tick them off as you go.
  • "Before you finish" shows which steps are left: tick them, choose "Not yet", or "Finish anyway".
  • The task's history records when it was finished.

Why it matters"Done" means done, and half-finished work doesn't quietly drop off the plan.

C

Keep & reuse

Nothing you write is wasted: hours become invoices, notes become flashcards, and every change can be undone.

07 · Billing

From hours worked to an invoice

Mark tasks as billable for a client, and the logged hours turn into a ready invoice.

  • Each client has its own rate; every line is hours × rate, then tax and the total.
  • Invoices are numbered automatically from Settings → Billing.
  • Print or save as PDF, or export a CSV; on the phone it downloads as a page to share.

Why it mattersFreelancers bill from the same record they plan in, with no spreadsheet in between.

08 · Revise

Notes that turn into flashcards

Write "question :: answer" on any line of a note, and it becomes a flashcard in Revise.

  • Revise shows today's cards one at a time; the menu badge counts what's due.
  • "Knew it" brings a card back after 1, 3, 7, 21, 45 and then 90 days.
  • "Forgot" brings it back tomorrow, so weak cards come round more often.

Why it mattersStudents study in the same app they plan in, and remember more with less time.

09 · Version history

Nothing lost

NineFlow keeps copies of your whole planner, so a mistake is never final.

  • A copy is saved when it opens, then every hour while you make changes.
  • The last 24 hours are kept hour by hour, then one copy a day for 30 days.
  • Restore the whole planner or bring back deleted tasks; tasks you "let go" stay restorable for 30 days.

Why it mattersPeople try things freely when they know they can undo them.
