Brand design

The brief

A mark that could only be Nexura.

01

Nexura Infotech had a name and a line, "Creating Intelligent Solutions", and nothing else.

Tech logos often end up as a generic letter in a box. The aim was a mark that could only belong to Nexura, built from the name itself, that still works in one colour, at small sizes and on everything from an ID card to an exhibition stand.

  • Built from the name

    A mark that comes from N-E-X-U-R-A, not a generic tech symbol.

  • Works everywhere

    Full colour, single colour and black and white; clear on a business card and on a stand.

  • Easy to reuse

    A simple colour and type system the team can apply without a designer.

The mark

Six letters, one hexagon.

02
  • A hexagon of folded strips

    Solid and dependable as a shape, with movement in the folds. Engineering, not magic.

  • NSix letters, one mark

    Each fold traces a letter: N, E, X, U, R and A can all be found inside the hexagon.

  • ▶A spark at the centre

    The dark triangle in the middle points forward: the "intelligent solution" moving out.

Colour, type & pattern

Four colours, one system.

03

Four colours, blue, green, red and orange, make up the full-colour mark. Each also works as a solid background with the logo reversed in white. The logo lettering is Sora SemiBold for NEXURA and Satoshi Regular for the tagline: clean geometric sans-serifs that sit comfortably next to the mark.

Website design & development

The brief

A website for careful buyers.

01

Nexura builds AI into the systems of banks, insurers, payment firms and pharmaceutical manufacturers, and deploys it on the client's own infrastructure.

With the brand in place, they needed a website that could stand in front of a bank's technology team. I designed it and built it myself.

The line that set the tone

Most enterprises do not need more AI. They need AI that earns its place.

From the Nexura site copy

That line set the direction. The people reading this site are careful by profession: a model that gets something wrong once is a failed model to them. So nothing here could look like an AI hype page. No glowing robots, no neon gradients. The design had to feel calm, exact and accountable, like the work Nexura promises.

Who it's for

Two industries, three people.

02

Two very different industries, and inside each one, people who judge a vendor on different things.

FintechBanking · Insurance · Payments

Bank technology headWill this pass our model-risk review, and who owns it when it breaks?
  • Governance
  • Audit trail

Process-drivenPharma · Process industry

Plant or quality leadOur systems are validated. I can't have anything touching uptime.
  • Works with what runs today
  • Uptime first

In both industriesDecision maker

How long until something is actually in production, and who do I talk to?
  • A timeline
  • A named person

Design goals

Four rules for every page.

03
  • Calm, not hype

    Plain language, quiet colour and real product moments instead of AI clichés. Confidence comes from restraint.

  • Two industries, never mixed up

    A bank and a pharma plant should each find their own path in one click, and always know which side they're on.

  • Proof before the pitch

    Numbers, named platforms and firm commitments come before any request to get in touch.

  • A person at the end

    The goal of every page is one conversation with a named principal, not a newsletter signup.

From brand to website

The logo colours, tuned for screens.

04

Amber, green and red come straight from the logo, at their exact values. Blue is the one change: buttons and links need a brighter blue on white, so the interface uses #1D4FF5 for every action and highlight, while the logo itself keeps its own #266EB4. Amber marks everything process-driven, and deep navy panels give the page its rhythm.

Key design decisions

Six choices that shaped the site.

07

Pick one to see it on the live page.

Hero

Show the product working, not a robot

Instead of an AI illustration, the hero shows a co-pilot doing a real job: drafting a credit memo. It ticks through its steps, cites every figure, and stops at a human "Approve" button. One picture explains the whole offer: AI does the work, people sign it off. The headline also rotates between "AI Integration" and "AI Co-pilots", the two things Nexura sells.

Navigation

A menu organised the way a bank buys

Fourteen products would be a wall of names. The Solutions menu groups them by what a buyer is shopping for: Payments, Banking operations, Audit & compliance and Data, with the process-driven AI apps in their own column. Each item carries its short code (IPS, BLOS, BRECON) and a one-line description, so it reads like a product catalogue, not a sitemap.

Where we work

Two doors, two colours

The home page splits early into two cards: Fintech in blue, Process-driven in amber. Each says who it's for and why that industry is hard, then shows its icons and a count. From here on, the colour tells the visitor which side they're on.

Solutions

Ten platforms you can scan in seconds

Every platform gets the same small card: an icon, a name and one plain line of what it does ("Account-to-account transfers that settle in seconds"). No jargon paragraphs. A buyer can scan all ten and spot the one they came for.

Commitments

The one dark moment

Most of the site is light and open. The commitments are the exception: three numbered promises on deep navy, with the key words picked out in blue. The change of colour slows the reader down at the part Nexura most wants remembered.

Engagement

Three ways to work, in three words

"We own. We pair. We guide." Each model gets a two-word headline and a "best when" line, so a client can pick the one that fits their team. A navy strip underneath repeats the promise every model shares: a first production cut within ninety days.

Inside pages

Same kit, every page.

08

Every inner page uses the same kit as the home page: a short promise, a small product card that shows the work, and a clear next step.

On the phone

Designed for the phone, not squeezed.

09

The number band becomes a 2×2 grid, the platform grid becomes a single column, and the menu turns into a full-screen list with Solutions folding open and a "Start a conversation" button always at the bottom.

Building it

Built by hand, shipped as designed.

10

Because I designed and built it, nothing was lost in a handoff. No page builder, no framework: just fast, plain web pages.

  • Plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript25 static pages, one stylesheet and one small script of about 21 KB for all the interaction.

  • Design tokensColours, type, radii and shadows live as CSS variables at the top of one stylesheet, so every page shares one system.

  • Self-hosted fontsTASA Orbiter and Inter are served from the site itself, so nothing loads from a third party.

  • Living detailsA rotating audience line in the top bar, the rotating headline, the co-pilot card and the platform ticker.

  • Gentle motionSections rise in as they scroll into view, and all motion switches off for people who prefer reduced motion.

  • Accessible menusThe mega menu and the mobile menu work with a keyboard and announce their state to screen readers.

  • FindableA unique title and description on every page, a sitemap of all 25 pages and an open robots file.
