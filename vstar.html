01

The brief

Built for the moment something breaks.

VSTAR repairs, services and maintains doors, roller shutters, high-speed doors, grills and windows for businesses across Singapore.

They needed two things: a logo, and a website that turns visitors into service requests. I designed both, then built the site myself.

A faulty door is not just a maintenance issue. It can slow operations, affect security and bring a business to a standstill.

From the VSTAR site copy, and the problem I designed around

That line changed how I saw the project. Nobody browses a shutter repair website for fun. The person on this page is usually standing in front of a door that won't open, on their phone, wondering who to call and what it will cost. Every decision below comes back to that person.

Design goals

Three goals, one thumb away.

  1. 01One tap to ask for help

    From any part of the page, on any screen, the way to reach VSTAR is never more than a thumb away.

  2. 02"Do they handle my door?"

    Answer it with pictures. People recognise their shutter by how it looks, not by its trade name.

  3. 03Earn trust before the call

    Answer the cost and reliability worries up front, so the enquiry starts with confidence, not doubt.

06

Key design decisions

Three screens, read up close.

Each screen carries numbered notes. Point at a note, or a pin on the screen, to link the two.

Hero

Answer the cost worry before it's asked

Beside the promise sit four plain guarantees: a 60-minute emergency response, a free site inspection, and no after-hours or weekend surcharges. These are the questions people hesitate over before they call, so the first screen answers them.

  1. Four guarantees with green ticks, right under the promise.
  2. A carousel card for each system VSTAR services, with its own link.
  3. Phone and WhatsApp in the header, on every screen.
Products

Show the door, not the trade name

Twelve systems as photo cards, with filter chips for Doors, Shutters & Grills, Fire & Smoke Safety and Windows. A visitor who doesn't know what a "perforated shutter" is can still find theirs by looking.

  1. Filter chips narrow the twelve systems instantly.
  2. Photo cards: people recognise their door by how it looks.
  3. A request bar closes the section, so help is right there.
Services

Every service has its own button

Repair, General Servicing and Annual Maintenance each get a card, a one-line promise and their own request button. When an enquiry arrives, VSTAR already knows what kind of job it is.

  1. Four short promises set the tone before the details.
  2. Each service gets a one-line promise in green.
  3. Its own request button, so every enquiry arrives labelled.
07

The enquiry flow

From "I need help" to WhatsApp.

The most important screen on the site isn't a page. It's the two steps between "I need help" and a message in VSTAR's WhatsApp.

The team already answers customers on WhatsApp, so I didn't replace it with a form that disappears into an inbox. The form only prepares the message. Two short steps collect what the team needs to quote, then WhatsApp opens with everything written out. The customer reads it, edits if they want, and sends.

08

On the phone

Mobile first, because that's where the door is.

I designed mobile first, because the person who needs a repair is usually standing next to the broken door with a phone in hand. The mobile layout keeps one green "WhatsApp Enquiry" bar fixed at the bottom of the screen, wherever you scroll.
