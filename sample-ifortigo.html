Case study · Logo + website

iFortigoLogo & website for a field sales app.

Outcome Shipped a logo and a one-page website, designed and built, where a sales manager can book a demo from anywhere on the page.

iFortigo Sales is an app for field sales and direct store delivery (DSD) teams: the people who drive the routes, book the orders and print the invoices at the shop counter. I designed its logo, then designed and built the one-page website that explains it.

01

The brief

A dense product, one easy scroll.

iFortigo does a lot: orders, invoices, returns, discounts, VAT, routes and attendance, in seven modules and 42 features. The people who choose it want one answer first: will this make my field team faster?

So the website had three jobs. Say what iFortigo is in one line. Make a long feature list easy to scan, so anyone who wants the detail can find it. And make every path on the page end in the same place: a demo request.

A field sales platform is used by one person, managed by another and paid for by a third. The page has something for each of them.

On the page: benefits for managers

06

Key design decisions

Six decisions, up close.

01

A promise and a picture

One sentence says what iFortigo is and who it is for, with a single Get Started button under it. The picture shows the same app in both of its colour themes, blue and purple. Just below, a strip answers the four things a buyer checks first: data security, SaaS cloud, ERP integration and 24/7 support.

02

Fifteen benefits, one glance

Every benefit is a tile with one icon and two or three words, five to a row. There is nothing to read in full: you see the range of the product in the time it takes to scroll past.

03

A spotlight that moves on its own

The business outcomes sit in one navy panel. One is shown large while the rest wait beside it. The spotlight moves on every 3.5 seconds while the panel is on screen, and holds still for six seconds when you point at an item.

04

Two audiences, one section

Field executives and their managers want different things. Instead of two pages, one section with two tabs: each person picks their own tab and reads only their own benefits.

05

42 features in 7 modules

The full feature list is grouped into seven numbered module cards. Their headers take turns in blue and purple, so neighbouring columns never blur into one, and the big number in each header gives every module a name you can point to.

06

Every road ends here

Every Book a Demo and Get Started button leads to one short form. Each button also writes its own name into a hidden field, so every enquiry says which button brought it in. The form sends without leaving the page, and a WhatsApp button waits in the corner for anyone who would rather chat.

07

Building it

Hand-built, nothing extra on board.

I built the site the way I designed it: one page of plain code, with no framework and no page builder.

Manifestifortigo.com · index.html84 KB
  • One page

    About 84 KB of HTML, with its styles and one 10 KB script inside. The only outside files are the two fonts.

  • Design tokens

    Colours, radii, shadows and both fonts live as variables at the top of the stylesheet.

    --primary: #0A409A--secondary: #7F13B7--radius-md: 18px--font-head: Sora
  • One icon set, on brand

    The line icons are inline SVG. A short script gives every one of them the same blue-to-purple gradient.

  • Calm motion

    Sections rise in as they reach the screen, and the header picks up a soft shadow after the first 20 pixels of scrolling.

  • The spotlight

    Moves on every 3.5 seconds, only while it is on screen, and pauses for 6 seconds when someone points at an item.

  • Enquiries

    The form sends in the background through Web3Forms and shows a sent or failed message without reloading. The name of the button that brought the visitor travels with it.

  • On the phone

    The links fold into a dropdown card behind a menu button, and the WhatsApp button stays in the corner.
